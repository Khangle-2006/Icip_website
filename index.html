Bridging 2D Efficiency and 3D Context

A memory-guided framework for knee MRI multi-label classification that keeps a fast 2D backbone while recovering volumetric context for ACL and meniscus tear diagnosis.

Abstract

Lightweight MRI diagnosis with explainable evidence.

Knee MRI diagnosis is difficult because ACL and meniscus injuries may be subtle, sparse, and visually close to surrounding soft tissue. Full 3D models can capture context, but they are often expensive and harder to deploy.

SingleViewMemoryNet bridges this gap by combining multi-scale center crops, a RadImageNet-pretrained 2D backbone, depth-aware attention, and task-aware memory. The result is a compact model that predicts ACL and meniscus tears while highlighting the anatomical evidence behind its decisions.

Task Multi-label knee MRI

ACL tear and meniscus tear prediction from single-view MRI sequences.

Backbone Frozen ResNet-18

RadImageNet initialization limits trainable parameters and overfitting.

Memory Task-specific prototypes

Separate ACL and meniscus banks retrieve diagnostic visual patterns.

Evidence Grad-CAM + IoU

Attention maps align with lesion regions annotated by physicians.

Method

Single-view speed, volumetric reasoning.

Full architecture

MRI slices pass through a RadImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18 with CBAM, are aggregated by depth attention, and then enter a task-aware memory module. ACL and meniscus features are refined separately before the final multi-label classifier.

Multi-scale center crops

Global, mid-level, and close-up views mimic radiologists zooming toward anatomy.

Depth-aware attention

Slice weights emphasize informative frames instead of averaging all slices equally.

Task-aware memory

Separate memory banks reduce interference between ACL and meniscus evidence.

Multi-label output

ACL and meniscus probabilities are predicted together while preserving task-specific evidence paths.

Depth attention module

Spatial GAP creates slice descriptors, a bottleneck estimates attention scores, and the reweighted volume is summed into a compact 2D feature map.
